01 / 08

Investigation 1 · Four advantages compound in Strata’s favor.

01
Fact

Adaptive expert residency

Niko1221/Strata keeps hot experts in VRAM and reports near-100% cache hit in third-party telemetry. The Laguna fork’s static split leaves an estimated 78% of expert work on the Threadripper.

Why it dominates: the choice is near-zero expert transfer, ≈1.9 GB of RAM reads plus CPU compute, or ≈2.44 GB over PCIe each step.
02
Fact

Smaller, more skewed Qwen experts

Qwen experts are ≈4.92M parameters each; Laguna experts are ≈9.44M. A few thousand cached Qwen experts cover most routed work, while Laguna’s top-10-of-256 routing gives each VRAM gigabyte less reach.

Structural: an engine change cannot make Laguna’s experts smaller or give it Qwen’s routing distribution.
03
Fact

O(1) attention in ¾ of Qwen layers

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next uses Gated DeltaNet linear attention for three of every four layers. Laguna has 12 global full-attention layers whose KV cache grows with context.

Long-context consequence: corrected KV math puts total target KV at ≈6.07 GiB at 128k in BF16, before weights, buffers, draft, or expert cache.
04
Hypothesis

DFlash is likely an active regression

Three independent community measurements reviewed in the research found poolside-fork DFlash 2–4× slower than no draft on VRAM-constrained CUDA. Likely cause: verification multiplies CPU expert FLOPs while the 2.2 GB BF16 draft displaces cached experts.

Do not infer—measure: run the same greedy 200-token request with DFlash off and on, then read accepted draft length from the server log.

Primary architecture sources: Niko1221/Strata, Laguna S 2.1 model card, and Poolside GGUF card. DFlash community measurements came from the dushyant30suthar/opencode-llama.cpp, njbrake/laguna-otari-bridge, and hizrianraz/laguna-s-2.1-spark-agentic reports reviewed on GitHub.

Investigation 2 · CPU fusion failed for the right reason.

At batch 1, IQ2_S expert GEMV is a streaming problem. Gate/up fusion removes neither weight bytes nor dequantization; it saves only a second read of a 6 KB activation beside roughly 2.01 MB of gate/up weights.

A

Do not retry gate/up fusion

A correct fused kernel cannot remove the dominant bytes. If either candidate regressed, inspect only three failure modes: FP16 temporary materialization, lost SIMD vec-dot, or broken row work-splitting.

Decision: ruled out unless a microbenchmark proves current vec-dot reaches less than 70% of STREAM.
B

Tune NUMA and MoE threads

Sweep -t 16/24/32, --numa interleave/distribute, and GGML_CPU_MOE_OFFLOAD_THREADS. SMT can hurt a bandwidth-bound GEMV, and a known tiny-graph heuristic has single-threaded this path in other forks.

Expected: ±10–20%. Prove with tok/s plus per-core utilization; kill if cores and both memory nodes are already saturated.
C

Keep cold routed experts on CPU

Streaming all cold experts over the measured 6.6 GB/s link imposes a ≈215 ms/token floor. Only the shared expert and hot routed experts belong on GPU.

Placement check: verify shared experts are CUDA-resident in the load log; they are touched every token.
D

Right-size DFlash verification

At n-max 15 and mean acceptance ≈4.5, estimated distinct-expert traffic is ≈17.1 GB per verify step—about 62 ms per accepted token. At n-max 4 and third-party acceptance ≈3.4, the model estimates ≈30 ms/token.

Critical caveat: these are estimates that happen to reproduce 16.1 tok/s; local acceptance metrics decide.

Model and draft configuration: target config.json and DFlash config.json. Third-party DFlash measurements: Laguna ROCmFP4 STRIX GGUF and DFlash S-tier finding.

05 / 08

Overlap cache refreshes—without publishing a half-filled slot.

The proposed reorder is sound only for a hot-GPU / cold-CPU design. If the local binary uses upstream’s synchronous miss-upload cache, the design itself—not just its timing—must change on a 6.6 GB/s link.

Run concurrently. Host DMA and CPU reads share DDR4, so overlap can slow the cold chain ≈10–15% during upload windows.
4 · Publish slot map
Hazard gate
Expose the new mapping only after ev_up: stream-wait before next hot launch or synchronize at the next planner step.

Pinned-buffer lifetime

Process

Model weights must remain page-locked for the whole process. No per-step allocation or free.

Double buffering

Not needed

LRU hits move to MRU before eviction; the in-flight hot chain never reads a refilling slot, and publication waits on ev_up.

06 / 08

Run the cheap tests before writing engine code.

Highest leverage

Sweep DFlash n-max: off, 4, 7, 15

Use one prompt, greedy decoding, and 200 generated tokens for every point. Research estimates 15 can reproduce the current 16.1 tok/s while 4 could approach the 30 tok/s budget.

Proof / kill: decode tok/s plus mean accepted length. If acceptance stays below 2.5, disable DFlash; if off wins, keep it off.
Potential +25–45%

Test a tighter expert quant

A UD-IQ2_XS-class build could reduce CPU bytes and let VRAM hold more experts. It may also reduce quality.

Proof / kill: same prompts and flags, plus a reasoning-loop quality probe. Throughput without coherent completion does not count.
Potential +10–30%

Swap only the AVX2 CPU backend

Try a compatible ik_llama.cpp-class or newer kernel build without changing the GGUF or runtime flags.

Proof / kill: same model file, prompt, and settings. First confirm the candidate binary supports Laguna S 2.1; upstream PR #25165 covers XS.2 / M.1, not S.2.1.
Potential +5–10%

If DFlash stays, move its draft to CPU

Freeing the roughly 2.2 GB BF16 draft from VRAM may improve expert residency by about 5%.

Proof / kill: record VRAM before and after, then compare decode tok/s and draft acceptance.
Prefill +10–20%

Tune batch and KV settings

For the 779 → 900 prefill target, test -ub 512 → 1024 → 2048, confirm flash attention, and test K-cache Q8_0.

Proof / kill: prefill tok/s on one fixed 8k prompt; retain only settings that do not harm decode.

Investigation 3 · 128k needs a deliberate KV/cache trade.

Corrected target-KV budget: 6.07 GiB BF16 at 128k.

The 36 sliding-window layers are genuinely bounded to 512 tokens in the poolside path; only 12 global layers grow with context. BF16 still pushes the estimated full VRAM footprint to ≈18.3 GB, so 128k needs Q8_0/Q4_0 KV and/or a smaller expert cache. Keep --swa-full off.
